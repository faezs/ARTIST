SO, PRECISELY: the two mounts are NOT the same machine, and they do not need to be. Hashemi's arc rail is a two-parameter
remote-centre mount whose centre IS F: it reaches its whole band at beta 0 and needs no beta at all. The pedicel is a
five-joint arm whose retro reach is a lobe about its own meridian - 25 % of the rail's zone - so at beta 0 it serves only
180 of 479 suns. What closes the gap is not a control law and not a longer boom alone: it is that the head's axis is
free. Off retro the head may stand anywhere on a cap of directions about the anti-sun line, and the union of those caps
over beta <= 45 deg covers every sun of the year: 479/479 = 100 %. The register sheet states what it costs (beta_flux.py):
beta is not a tax here up to 36 deg - it is a gain of 29-35 %, because it takes the pipe and the fold strip off the aperture -
and only the last 14 suns, worth 3.0 % of the year's energy, ask for beta past 36, at 2 % of themselves.
